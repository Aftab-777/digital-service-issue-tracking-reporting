# Assumptions and questions for real stakeholders

## Working assumptions in this simulation

- One row represents one request, with a unique request ID.
- The status list has four values; Completed is the only closed status.
- Target completion dates are internal planning dates, not service-level commitments.
- Overdue means a noncompleted request with a target date earlier than the visible report date.
- Priority labels are examples; no formal impact/urgency matrix was supplied.
- Role labels are fictional and carry no authority to approve access or policy changes.
- The reporting snapshot is 7 October 2026. CSV and reporting pack are fixed snapshots; Excel formulas change with edits to the workbook.

## Questions to confirm before real use

1. Which team owns the tracker and approves changes to status definitions?
2. What makes a request high priority, and who can change priority?
3. Which target dates are commitments, and how should extensions be recorded?
4. What evidence is needed before marking a request Completed?
5. How should reopened or cancelled requests be represented?
6. Which people may view sensitive request details and access notes?
7. What reporting cadence, archival period and version control are required?
8. What escalation path applies to overdue or blocked items?
9. Which fields are mandatory at intake, and who repairs incomplete records?
10. Should owner names, roles or both be stored in SharePoint?
