# Request Prioritization Brief

Decision note for a fictional operations manager | 7 October 2026

## Decision

Choose whether to trial a regular exception review before investing in automation.

## Evidence

22 of 36 requests are open. 9 are overdue and 6 are Blocked; the groups overlap. 6 open items are High priority. Two requests have no owner, and two need date or status review. DS-034's invalid target is included in overdue, so that figure is provisional.

## Options

1. Continue informal follow-up: little scheduled coordination, but exceptions may be missed. 2. Run a weekly owner-led review and repair flagged fields: creates a clear action record, with modest assumed effort. 3. Automate reminders now: potentially consistent prompts, but target rules and source quality are not yet settled.

## Recommendation

Trial option 2 for four weeks. Start with overdue and blocked records, assign missing owners, check DS-033 and DS-034 against source notes, and record decisions in Next action. Review the trial before considering automation.

## Why this helps decide

The briefing separates observable tracker exceptions from the proposed process change. A manager can approve or decline the trial, assign coordination responsibility and request corrections before treating the headline as performance evidence.

## Assumptions and risks

A 20-minute weekly meeting and four-week trial are proposed, not measured costs or benefits. A weekly review cannot itself resolve missing approvals or requester details; inconsistent dates may distort the exceptions list.

## Decision required

Approve or decline the trial and agree how target changes and unresolved blockers would be documented. This is a simulation; no manager has made the decision.

*Personal portfolio simulation. All requests and role names are synthetic.*
